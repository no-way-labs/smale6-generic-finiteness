# Referee R31 — Generic finiteness of planar central configurations

17 November 2026. I read [the second-read brief](as-read/R31-write-up.md) at commit `7421729`.

**Sound with fixes.** Lemma C’s corner argument holds, including when the lightest body occurs in four or more cycle pairs. The scheme-theoretic passage in Lemma D0 works when stated over the rational mass scheme with the source-side fibre dimension. Two 2026 preprints give proofs of the positive-real generic statement for six bodies. I found no Newtonian planar continuum at masses meeting D5′’s valuation hypothesis.

I used the brief, public literature, and the search linked below. My first Git query displayed the commit subject, which included an earlier referee’s verdict. I did not open either earlier report, any other referee’s scripts, or the repository literature note.

## S9 and Q1 — Literature and possible counterexamples

I searched the web and scholarly indexes for generic finiteness, relative equilibria, and continua of planar Newtonian central configurations. I followed results for six and seven bodies and arbitrary $n$, including signed, complex, massless, and small-mass variants. The status below reflects the public sources I could inspect on the report date.

I found no accessible, standing proof of unrestricted planar generic finiteness for any $n\ge7$. The two six-body preprints quantify over physical configurations. Tomar’s proof starts from a curve with $U\ne0$, obtained from physical points, and Chen–Tong start from a positive-point first limit. D5 and D5′ quantify over the full normalized algebraic system, with complex mass and inverse-distance choices. §0 should name both preprints, acknowledge the earlier all-$n$ claims, and state the scope of the new result. If “published” refers to journal publication, say so explicitly. [Tomar, Corollaries 2.4 and 2.7](https://www.mefyi.com/papers/smale-6-six-body-generic/smale-6-six-body-generic.pdf), [Chen–Tong, §§1–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.23329v1)

Several nearby results have narrower hypotheses. Jensen–Leykin prove the generic result via mass valuations for $n\le5$ and report nonpointed recession cones in their six-body run. Chang–Chen’s papers on diagrams and mass relations leave 24 six-body cases in their combined preprint. Dias–Pan prove generic finiteness within the cross-symmetric six-body class with $m_5=m_6$. Moczurad–Zgliczyński count the physical planar configurations for equal masses through $n=7$ and obtain counts on a real neighbourhood of equal masses. Xia counts configurations on some real open sets for arbitrary $n$. Such an open set can coexist with a Zariski-dense bad set elsewhere in the real mass space. Moeckel’s Dziobek theorem concerns affine dimension $n-2$, and Natrup–Wang–Wang bound the number of nondegenerate configurations. ### Continua and the valuation test

- **Roberts’ Newtonian rhombi.** At fixed masses $(1,1,1,1,-1/4)$, Roberts gives a continuum of inequivalent planar relative equilibria. Albouy–Kaloshin also give the **modified Roberts continuum** at $(4,4,4,4,1)$, using the negative distance root for every pair involving the centre. It is a continuum in $N_5$ modulo rotation. Changing the signs of the centre’s mass and those four roots preserves the outer-body equations, and multiplies the centre’s already vanishing force by $-1$. Tomar’s *Complex central configurations of the planar five-body problem at special masses*, Theorem 1.1, gives 36 curves in the normalized slice at the permuted mass vector $(1,4,4,4,4)$. Its Theorem 1.3 establishes $U=0$ on any curve at $(9,36,36,36,4)$ and its multiples and permutations, without asserting existence there. On each of these mass rays over $K$, a vector $s m^0$ with $s\ne0$ and $m^0$ the indicated rational vector has valuation $v(s)(1,\ldots,1)$, because every nonzero rational constant has valuation zero. Thus every listed mass ray misses the strict first gap of the chamber in every ordering. §8 should include the modified example since its equations and positive algebraic masses are within the scope of D5. [Roberts, Theorem 3.1](https://mathcs.holycross.edu/~groberts/Papers/continuum.pdf), [Albouy–Kaloshin, p. 543 and Remark 8](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v176-n1-p10-p.pdf), [Tomar, Theorems 1.1–1.3](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00485v1)
- **Families with varying masses and higher-dimensional examples.** Roberts’ nine-body example in his concluding remarks varies the equal masses on an inner square, so it describes points in different fibres and has repeated mass weights even along a series in that parameter. O’Neil’s Theorem 2 gives necessary conditions for a fixed-mass singular continuation, and Theorem 3 supplies continuations with varying masses. A series of the latter kind gives a configuration over the series field, while D5′ is a claim about the dimension of its fixed-mass fibre over that field. The Hachmeister–Little–McGhee–Pelayo–Sasarita extensions use two orthogonal copies of a configuration whose affine span has dimension at least two. The new continua therefore have affine span of dimension at least four. [Roberts, concluding remark 2](https://mathcs.holycross.edu/~groberts/Papers/continuum.pdf), [O’Neil](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physd.2013.12.001), [Hachmeister et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10569-013-9471-1), [Little’s account of the doubling construction](https://mathcs.holycross.edu/~little/JMMTalkCCs.pdf)
- **Other force laws and zero masses.** The Alfaro–Pérez-Chavela examples use Newtonian plus Coulomb pair coefficients $m_i m_j-e_i e_j$. Santoprete’s continuum uses a quadratic potential, discussed in Q2 below. A Newtonian massless control already exists in $N_n$ with $m_0>0$ and $m_1=\cdots=m_{n-1}=0$: for $n\ge3$, the test bodies can sit at distinct points on the circle of radius $m_0^{1/3}$ about body $0$. Their relative angles give infinitely many shapes, and their zero masses have valuation $+\infty$. [Alfaro–Pérez-Chavela](https://online.watsci.org/abstract_pdf/2002v9/v9n4a-pdf/1.pdf), [Santoprete](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.4904)

### Which ingredients have precedents?

Albouy–Kaloshin §2, equations (4), (8) and (9), gives the signed inverse-distance complexification and the complex rotation. Their Rule 1c uses the cancellation of internal forces in a mass-weighted subset sum. Their Lemma 1 and §9’s proof of Theorem 2 are the direct antecedent of D2. Given fixed mutual distances there are only finitely many normalized configurations, so on a continuum some squared distance has cofinite image and can be sent to zero. D2 implements this over $K$ by taking an element of sufficiently large valuation. Tomar §4.1 puts $O$ in both ultrametric trees and allows a body to occupy its leaf. His §9, just before Lemma 9.4, uses the same subset-sum identity as Lemma A on a singular place. [Albouy–Kaloshin](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v176-n1-p10-p.pdf), [Tomar, §§4.1 and 9](https://www.mefyi.com/papers/smale-6-six-body-generic/smale-6-six-body-generic.pdf)

Jensen–Leykin §§2.4 and 3.3 give the closest precedent for D0’s use of valued masses to prove a generic statement. They use Zariski density of a fixed mass-valuation fibre. The brief uses one mass vector with algebraically independent coordinates. Xia supplies a real small-mass precedent for using a hierarchy. I found no accessible antecedent for the specific maximum-$\Psi$ cycle exclusion in Lemma C or for D4’s mixed cycle forcing a nonzero coefficient of $\Psi^*$. [Jensen–Leykin](https://arxiv.org/html/2301.02305v2), [Xia](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0396(91)90137-X)

## S6 — Attempt to break Lemma C for $n\ge8$

### Proof check

1. **(C1)–(C3).** The two heaviest bodies are taken in $Z_e\cap W_e$. Substituting a third body $a$ for either endpoint holds both heights weakly above their old values, so maximality gives both $u_a\ge u_c$ and $u_a\ge u_d$. Strict weights exclude parallel maximizers. Along a body cycle, each colour’s heights cancel in the alternating sum. The resulting vector $q$ has sum zero, $|q_j|\le n-1$, and at most $n-2$ indices strictly between 0 and its last nonzero index. Lemma B forces $q=0$. On an odd path from $z$ to $w$, the endpoint heights both have sign $+$ and every interior height cancels. Concatenating one path with the reverse of the other gives the full alternating cycle, so the difference between the two $\sigma$ vectors is $\pm q$. In particular $\sum_k\sigma_k=2$, and $|\sigma_k|\le n-1$ by distinctness of the pairs.

2. **(C4) and the staircase.** For $p,p'\in Z\cap W$, their heights are bounded *below* by the two node heights, so their $\Psi$ is bounded below by $(h_z+g_w)/2-u_p-u_{p'}$. Maximality gives the displayed inequality (P). Any two chosen nodes in either colour containing $L$ are comparable. Simultaneous weak inclusions put the partner of one $L$-edge in the other’s intersection, contrary to (C1) since both partners are heavier than $L$. Equality in either chain is therefore impossible. The opposite strict inclusion chains follow.

3. **(C5)(b)–(c).** Orient the walk from $z$ to $w$ on odd edges. Crossing an $L$-edge in that direction reduces $\ell$ by one. The reverse crossing increases it by one. Balance closes the walk, and the change between any two nodes in either direction is bounded above by the total number $m/2$ of upward jumps. The coefficient of $1_L$ in $\sigma(z,w)$ telescopes to $\ell(z)-\ell(w)$ with this convention. In a corner $i<j$, take $p=x_i$ and $p'=x_j$ directly in (P). Both are in $Z_i\cap W_j$. Then $v=1_{x_i}+1_{x_j}-\sigma(z_i,w_j)$ has sum zero, coefficients bounded by $n$, and zero coefficients at every index above $L$, including bodies that lie off the cycle. If $\ell(z_i)-\ell(w_j)\ge1$, then $v_L\le-1$. With $R_k=u_k-u_0$, two heavier partners ensure $L\ge2$ and $R_{L-1}>0$. The corner estimate is
   $$
   v\cdot u\le -R_L+n(L-1)R_{L-1}
   <\bigl(n(n-2)-(1+\tau)\bigr)R_{L-1}\le0 .
   $$
   The last inequality holds for $\tau\ge n^2-2n-1$. The middle inequality remains strict at that threshold. This independently gives the sign and bound at lines 315–316 for arbitrary $u_0$.

4. **(C5)(d).** Adjacent corners alone force $\ell(w_{i+1})\ge\ell(w_i)+1$. Using $\ell(z_m)=\ell(w_m)+1$ gives a range of at least $m$, whereas the closed walk has only $m/2$ upward jumps. There is also a direct corner choice. Pick two crossings of the lowest level cut of the closed walk, one up and one down. Both cross at an $L$-edge and have the same $\ell(w)$. Their staircase ordering makes the earlier $z$ and later $w$ a corner with $\sigma_L=1$. This checks the obstruction without assuming any particular ordering of $L$-edges around the cycle.

### Exact search and worked examples

I generated balanced lists of distinct body pairs by alternating along closed circuits of body labels. The search permutes the pairs within their two parity classes. For each attempted bipartite cycle, set $h_{z_0}=\Psi^\circ=0$ by two height shifts. The equalities on the cycle then force all node heights by recursion. At each height, unite the endpoints of the two pairs incident to each proposed node. Processing heights in descending order gives the smallest pair heights required by the ultrametric inequalities. Any admissible model must at least contain these unions. A required cycle edge already joined at a higher height, or two prescribed same-colour nodes forced to coincide, rejects the proposed cycle. The code checks the resulting intersections and then looks for a corner or another pair with $\Psi>0$. All calculations use integers with $u_0=0$ and $u_j=(n^2+2)^{j-1}$ for $j\ge1$.

The run tested **653,600 placements** across $n=8,9,10,11,12,13$. It included all 2,520 label orders of an eight-body circuit modulo rotation and reversal, with 144 alternating placements each. Other patterns exercised repeated appearances of $L$ up to eight times, and one case had a lighter body off the cycle. Of the placements meeting the forced heights, simple-node constraints, and (C1), 544 eight-body placements and four nine-body placements reached the corner check. Every one was rejected by an adjacent corner. The search covers those specified patterns and one chamber vector per size. It also recovered a genuine four-edge balanced maximum cycle when run with equal weights on pairs of bodies outside the chamber. To reproduce, run `python3 -I -B data/n7/allN/hier/r31/search_balanced_cycles.py` from the repository root. [Search script](../data/n7/allN/hier/r31/search_balanced_cycles.py), [output](../data/n7/allN/hier/r31/search_balanced_cycles.txt)

Two attempted constructions can be checked directly. Write $ab$ for $(a,b)$ and start each list with the edge from $z_0$ to $w_0$, followed by the one from $w_0$ to $z_1$.

- **Eight bodies, two appearances of $L$.** The order is $01,12,23,57,45,34,67,06$. The least admissible nodes for $z_2,z_3,w_3,w_1$ are $\{4,5,7\}$, $\{3,4,5,6,7\}$, $\{0,6,7\}$ and $\{0,2,3,5,6,7\}$. The $67$ edge at $(z_3,w_3)$ comes first in the staircase. Its corner with the $57$ edge at $(z_2,w_1)$ contains $5,6$. The path $67,06,01,12,23$ from $z_3$ to $w_1$ gives $\sigma=1_7+1_3$, so $\Psi(5,6)\ge u_7+u_3-u_5-u_6>0$. The body pair $56$ therefore beats the eight tied pairs.
- **Nine bodies, four appearances of $L$.** The order is $58,57,78,02,04,48,12,68,36,13$. It passes the forced-height and (C1) checks. The $48$ edge at $(z_3,w_2)$ precedes $78$ at $(z_1,w_1)$ in the staircase. The path $48,04,02$ from $z_3$ to $w_1$ gives $\sigma=1_8+1_2$. Thus the pair $47$ has $\Psi(4,7)\ge u_8+u_2-u_4-u_7>0$. At the search weights the lower bound for twice this excess was exactly $53{,}618{,}220{,}089{,}108$.

I would change the (C1) heading to “A pair of $E^\circ$ consists of the two heaviest bodies in the intersection of its nodes.” I would also specify the hypotheses of the ancillary six-body census. The sentence “A balanced simple cycle needs at least six bodies” is false for arbitrary weight data. The outside-chamber control uses the four active bodies $0,1,2,3$ with weights $(0,0,1,1)$, a $z$-cluster $\{1,2,3\}$ and a $w$-cluster $\{0,2,3\}$ of height 2, and roots of height 0 on those four bodies. All six pairs tie and $01,12,23,03$ is a balanced simple cycle. The no-cycle result for the chamber follows independently of any small-$n$ census.

## S3 — Scheme-theoretic check of Lemma D0

Let $A=\mathbb Q[m_0,\ldots,m_{n-1}]$, $S=\operatorname{Spec}A$, and $f:\mathcal X\to S$ be the affine finite-presentation scheme cut out by (N1)–(N3) over $A$. Collision-freeness needed here is already part of (N1). All the dimension tests concern the underlying spaces of the fibres.

- **(ii), source-side semicontinuity.** Define $Z$ to be the reduced closed subscheme of $\mathcal X$ supported on $\{x:\dim_x\mathcal X_{f(x)}\ge2\}$. The dimension used here is the minimum of the dimensions of open neighbourhoods of $x$ in its fibre. At a closed point over an algebraically closed field it agrees with the local-ring dimension. At a generic point these differ, for example for the generic point of $\mathbb A^2$ the local ring has dimension zero. Stacks 02FZ gives closedness of the displayed source locus and 01J3 supplies its reduced scheme structure, both over $\mathbb Q$. By 02FY(1) its support pulls back to the same dimension locus after base change. This is the precise interpretation of “local dimension” at line 171. [Stacks 02FX](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02FX), [02FZ, also EGA IV$_3$ 13.1.3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02FZ), [01J3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01J3), [02FY](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/02FY)
- **(ii), constructible image and geometric points.** Take $B=f(|Z|)$ inside $|S|$. Since $Z\to S$ is affine of finite presentation, Chevalley applies. The topological image after any field base change is the inverse image of $B$, by the fibre-product point criterion in 0495. Over a closed $\mathbb C$-point the fibre of $Z_{\mathbb C}$ is nonempty exactly when it has a $\mathbb C$-point, by the Nullstellensatz. In a fibre of $\mathcal X_{\mathbb C}$, any component of dimension at least two contains a closed point of that local fibre dimension. Thus the closed $\mathbb C$-points of the pulled-back $B$ are precisely the bad mass vectors by (i). [Stacks 054K, also EGA IV 1.8.4](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/054K), [0495](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0495), [00FV](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00FV)
- **(iii), generic fibre.** Zariski density of the bad closed complex points implies density of $B$ in $S$. Any nonzero rational polynomial vanishing on $B$ would vanish on every bad complex point. A dense constructible set in the irreducible scheme $S$ contains its generic point $\eta$. Then $Z_\eta$ is nonempty and $\dim\mathcal X_\eta\ge2$. For $F=\kappa(\eta)=\mathbb Q(m_0,\ldots,m_{n-1})$, passing to $\bar F$ preserves dimension. A geometric generic fibre of dimension at least two therefore has an irreducible component of that dimension or higher. This spells out which generic point is used at line 176. [Stacks 005K](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/005K), [00P3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00P3)
- **(iv), Hahn embedding and component.** The $\mathbb Q$-linear independence of $u^*$ sends distinct mass monomials to distinct exponents. This gives an injection $A\hookrightarrow K$, hence $F\hookrightarrow K$. The field algebraic closure of the embedded $F$ inside $K$ is algebraically closed since roots of polynomials over it are algebraic over $F$. Thus the embedding extends to $\bar F$. Here $K=\overline{\mathbb Q}((T^{\mathbb R}))$ is algebraically closed by the Hahn-field result in Poonen, Theorem 1 and Corollary 4. Over the algebraically closed $\bar F$, the chosen component is geometrically irreducible, and its base change remains a component of the fibre by 04KW. Dimension is preserved by 00P3. The pullback of the original equations along $A\to K$ is exactly the fibre at the stated masses. [Poonen, *Maximally Complete Fields*](https://math.mit.edu/~poonen/papers/amsval.pdf), [Stacks 09GU](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/09GU), [020J](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/020J), [04KW](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/04KW), [00P3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00P3)

For (i), there is a global affine slice $H$ given by $X_{01}=1$. The action identifies $\mathcal X\simeq\mathbb G_m\times H$ over $S$. The inverse sends a solution $p$ to $(X_{01}(p),X_{01}(p)^{-1}\cdot p)$, which is regular since $X_{01}^{-1}=P_{01}^2Y_{01}$ in the coordinate ring. The product gives the claimed dimension shift, and $H_s$ over an algebraically closed field has infinitely many points exactly when it has positive dimension. This also supplies the dimension assertion about $V_1$ in D2. [Stacks 00FV](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00FV)

For §8’s rational closure, the scheme-theoretic image of $Z\to S$ is cut out by $\ker(A\to\Gamma(Z,\mathcal O_Z))$. It commutes with the flat extensions $\mathbb Q\subset\mathbb C,K$, and its support is the closure of the image. The closed points of $Z_{\mathbb C}$ are dense, so the support over $\mathbb C$ gives precisely the closure of the bad complex mass vectors. The same construction over $K$ gives the closure of the bad $K$-points. [Stacks 01R8](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/01R8), [081I](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/081I), [00FV](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00FV)

For the optional tropical remark, restrict this closure to the mass torus over $K$. The bad constructible set contains a dense open in each irreducible component of its closure. If a component’s tropicalization met the open chamber, there would be an actual valuation vector in the chamber on that component. Payne’s density of its valuation fibre gives a bad point in the chamber, contrary to D5′. Cite Corollary 4.2 together with the published correction that repairs the proof of its input, Theorem 4.1. [Payne, *Fibers of tropicalization*](https://arxiv.org/abs/0705.1732), [correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1204.1837)

**Q1.** Yes for $n=6$ at the level of explicit preprint proofs of §0, by Tomar and by Chen–Tong. For arbitrary $n$, Tibboel’s claim was withdrawn and Okolo’s public title claim has restricted contents. I found no accessible standing unrestricted planar generic proof for $n\ge7$. The antecedents for the complexification, cut sums, distance-to-zero step and use of valued masses are given above.

**Q2.** The quadratic pair potential in Santoprete’s example has force proportional to the separation. Let $M=\sum_jm_j$. The natural normalization at $M\ne0$ gives $M X_t=\sum_{l\ne t}m_l X_{tl}$ and its $Y$ analogue, so every centred collision-free shape is a solution.
