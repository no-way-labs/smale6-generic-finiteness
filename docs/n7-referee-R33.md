## F1. Definition 3.1, Lemma 3.2 and its uses

The extension to repeated indices is valid in the ordered set $\mathbb R\cup\{+\infty\}$. If $x=y$, the right side is $d_{xz}$. If $y=z$, it is also $d_{xz}$. If $x=z$, the left side is $+\infty$. These cases include the all-equal triple. For distinct indices the definition allows $+\infty$ on either side. In particular, two infinite entries on the right force the third entry to be infinite.

- In (a), $d_{xx}=+\infty$ and $d_{xy}=h$ put both generators in $A$. The comparison with the ball about $y$ uses $\min(h,d_{xp})=h$ for $p\in A$, including $p=x,y$ and $d_{xp}=+\infty$. An internal pair has $d_{pp'}\ge h$ even if $p=p'$. The same internal-pair inequality at the second generating height gives the reverse inequality needed for uniqueness of the height.
- In (b), the relation can be written $d_{pp'}>h$ because $h$ is real and the diagonal is infinite. Symmetry and the ultrametric inequality give transitivity even with infinite entries. Across distinct children the value is bounded below by $h$ and above by $h$, so it is real and equal to $h$.
- In (c), $d_{xp'}<h$ because $p'\notin A$. With $d_{xp}\ge h$, any $d_{pp'}\ge h$, including $+\infty$, contradicts that inequality. The argument includes $p=x$.
- In (d), $d_{pp'}=h$ is real, so $p\ne p'$ and their node is defined. Since $d_{xp}=d_{px}\ge h$, the two inequalities carry membership at threshold $h$ in both directions between the balls about $x$ and $p$. They apply when $p=x$, $q=p$, $q=x$, or $d_{xp}=+\infty$. This is the fixed-threshold proof R32 requested.

After Definition 5.1, equality of nodes of finite-height edges implies equality of heights by (a). In Theorem 6.2 Step 1, the endpoints of the maximizing edge occupy different children. Choose the child $S$ omitting $O$. For a cut term $\{p,p'\}$, the second endpoint is either in another child, with height $h$, or outside the node, with height less than $h$. Thus an included $O$ term has finite height in the other colour as well. This justifies the sum $\Psi(g)+\omega_g$ even though Definition 3.3 allows cut terms whose $\Psi$ elsewhere is $-\infty$. Equality forces $g\in E^*$ and gives the same $w$ node by (d), including if $p$ is a generator of the node. The argument with the colours exchanged has the same hypotheses.

Step 3 uses the ultrametric restrictions to the bodies and the height version of Lemma 5.2. A sequence made solely of body pairs implies $\Psi^\circ=\Psi^*$ and survives restriction as a closed alternating sequence. The step needs only equality of heights, so no separate comparison of nodes after removing $O$ is needed.

## F4. Remark 8.2 and Payne's corollary

1. Write $\Sigma=Z_1\cup\cdots\cup Z_r$ as the finite union of irreducible components. The open subset of $Z_i$ obtained by removing the other components is also open in $\Sigma$. Its intersection with $D$ is dense and constructible. Hence the generic point of $Z_i$ belongs to $D$. The constructible complement $\Sigma\setminus D$ cannot be dense on that same open subset, since density there would likewise force it to contain the generic point. Thus $U=\Sigma\setminus\overline{\Sigma\setminus D}$ is open, contained in $D$, and dense on every $Z_i$. If $D$ is empty, the claim is vacuous. [Stacks 005K](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/005K)
2. Give $\Sigma$ its reduced closed subscheme structure. First restrict it to the torus where all the $m_i$ are units, then extend scalars to $K$. Restriction and base change commute. On the restricted scheme, extension to $K$ is flat. By going down a minimal prime upstairs contracts to a minimal prime downstairs. Consequently each irreducible component of the torus part over $K$ dominates a surviving original component and its generic point lies in $U_K$. This proves that $U_K$ meets each component in a nonempty open subset even if an original component splits over $K$. A component lying in a coordinate hyperplane has empty intersection with the torus before and after scalar extension. The paper's component assertion is correct. Adding “by flatness and going down” would give the reader its justification. [Stacks 0CB3](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/0CB3)
3. Apply Payne to one such irreducible component with its reduced structure as a closed subvariety of the torus. The Hahn field is algebraically closed and its value group is $G=\mathbb R$, so $N_G=\mathbb R^n$ for this torus. Irrational coordinates are included. The assumed $K$-point makes the classical valuation fibre nonempty and gives a point of the initial degeneration by scaling each coordinate by the corresponding monomial and reducing the resulting units. Corollary 4.2 gives density of that valuation fibre in the component, and Remark 2 of the erratum gives the corrected justification for the classical fibre statement. [Payne, §2.1 and Corollary 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0705.1732#page=6) · [Erratum, Remark 2](https://web.ma.utexas.edu/users/sampayne/pdf/CorrectionToFibers.pdf)
4. The dense fibre intersects the nonempty open $U_K$ on the component. Its image below lies in $U\subset D$, so Lemma 8.1(a) gives infinitely many orbits at a mass vector where Theorem 1.3 gives finitely many. Equivariance under relabelling handles every permuted chamber. The argument therefore excludes the open chambers from the torus valuation image. Its Euclidean closure also avoids them because their complement is closed.

For the last counterexample sentence, specify that the infinitely many orbits occur at the indicated mass point, and that any embedding into $K$ is valuation-preserving. Distinct orbits of solutions over the smaller field stay distinct over $K$. Any scaling relating two such solutions must have parameter $X'_{01}/X_{01}$ in the smaller field. ## F9. Exact search and Section 13

The paragraph gives the correct vector and scope. The first gap is $1$ and for $j\ge2$ the gap is $(n^2+1)u_{j-1}>n^2(u_{j-1}-u_0)$. An order type records the relative order of all distinct real ultrametric heights, including ties between disjoint clusters. Its threshold relations are a strict chain of partitions. The search quotients the pair of colour orders by exchange, keeping an order type paired with itself. At $n=6$ it therefore tests $9{,}012\cdot9{,}013/2=40{,}612{,}578$ pairs.

The source makes a bipartite multigraph on **height ranks**. A distinct-edge closed alternating sequence gives a closed trail in it and contains a simple cycle, allowing parallel edges. Conversely, any simple cycle of maximizing pairs in the rank graph gives a closed alternating sequence as defined in §5, even if shared ranks come from different nodes. The source prunes pairs guaranteed to have strictly smaller $\Psi$ than another pair.
