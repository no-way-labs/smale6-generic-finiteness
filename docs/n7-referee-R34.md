- **Lemma 2.1.** Multiplication by $m_t\overline{z_t-c}$ and summation make the two contributions of each pair equal $m_tm_l/r_{tl}$ in total. Then $\lambda I=U>0$ and $\lambda>0$. Scaling by $\lambda^{1/3}$ gives the normalization. The mass-weighted sum fixes the center of mass. - **Lemma 2.2.** The substitution gives (1.2)–(1.4), and conjugation forces $s\bar s=1$ since some position is nonzero. The map is equivariant and injective on classes under physical rotations. Quotienting these classes by reflection preserves finiteness.
- **Lemmas 2.3 and 2.4.** Equation (1.2) makes all three factors nonzero, and $X_{01}$ detects the rotation. For an irreducible component $C$, the closure of the image of $\mathbb G_m\times C$ is irreducible and contains $C$, so it equals $C$. Every orbit is one-dimensional. Zero-dimensional components are therefore impossible. Two orbits in the same one-dimensional component would each contain an open subset and would meet. A finite union of orbits has dimension at most one.
- **Definition 3.1 and Lemma 3.2.** Poonen's result applies to the divisible value group $\mathbb R$ and algebraically closed residue field $\overline{\mathbb Q}$. The ultrametric inequality proves the two descriptions of a node and recovers its height as the minimum on it. In part (d), applying the inequality in both directions to $x,p,q$ indeed gives the same threshold set. Infinite values on origin pairs cause no issue. [Poonen, Theorem 1 and Corollary 4](https://math.stanford.edu/~conrad/Perfseminar/refs/poonencomplete.pdf)
- **Definition 3.3 and Lemma 3.4.** Both colors inherit ultrametric inequalities from the valuation. For either cut, the two orientations of an internal edge cancel. The valuation of an $X$-crossing term is $u_t+u_l-\zeta_{tl}/2-3\omega_{tl}/2=-\Phi^z$. A proper nonempty cut has a nonzero crossing term. The full cut has a nonzero position term by Lemma 2.3 and $n\ge2$. The minimum valuation is thus attained twice in each color. The origin terms are admitted separately for each cut, while membership in $E^*$ requires both origin heights to be finite.
- **Example 3.5.** Subtracting the $X$ equations gives $P_{01}^3(m_0+m_1)=1$. The positions satisfy $X_0=m_1X_{01}/(m_0+m_1)$ and $X_1=-m_0X_{01}/(m_0+m_1)$, with the same relations in $Y$. Then $v(m_0+m_1)=u_0$ gives the stated heights, maximum, and coincident nodes.

### G6 — Remark 1.4

Fix the masses over an algebraically closed field. There are $D=2n+\binom n2$ variables and $D$ equations once (1.2) is taken once per unordered pair. Its degree is 4. In each position equation the right side has degree at most 4 and the left side is linear. The slice adds the degree-one equation $X_{01}-1=0$. Equation (1.2) makes $X_{01}\ne0$, and the sole rotation giving $X_{01}=1$ uses $s=X_{01}^{-1}$. Heintz's Theorem 1, p. 251, gives $\deg(A\cap B)\le\deg A\deg B$ for constructible subsets of affine space. Iterating it on the hypersurfaces gives degree at most $4^D\cdot1$. For a finite reduced algebraic set this degree is the number of geometric points. The empty slice has zero points. Both fields of Theorems 1.2–1.3 meet the algebraic-closure hypothesis. Add this scope, “exactly one point,” and the theorem number to the remark. [Heintz, publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3975(83)90002-6), [full text, p. 251](https://www.academia.edu/76016550/Definability_and_fast_quantifier_elimination_in_algebraically_closed_fields)

### Additional search and counterexamples

On 18 November 2026 I searched for planar or Newtonian generic finiteness, seven-body or arbitrary $n$, Zariski-open masses, and continua of central configurations. I also searched arXiv, Zenodo, and other public records. The queries returned the four- and five-body results and both cited six-body preprints, which control the terms of the search. I found no accessible proof of unrestricted planar generic finiteness at any $n\ge7$ and no additional public claim of that scope. Natrup–Wang–Wang, Theorem 2.1, bounds **non-degenerate** planar configurations for arbitrary $n$. Dias's results concern dimension $n-2$. They are related restricted results and do not supply a missing broad row. [Natrup–Wang–Wang](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.20228), [Dias 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.06593), [Dias 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.07962)

The continuum queries recovered Roberts' example, its signed-root complex counterpart, and Little's higher-dimensional extensions with a negative mass. Roberts' example is at a fixed exceptional mass vector. The positive-mass signed-root example concerns $\mathcal N_5$ and its mass vector necessarily belongs to the exceptional locus in Theorem 1.2. These constant nonzero masses have equal Hahn valuation. The massless example excludes the required nonzero masses, and the polynomial-potential examples change the Newtonian force. I found no continuum at separated Hahn valuations or over a Zariski-dense set of mass vectors for the planar Newtonian complexified system. [Roberts](https://mathcs.holycross.edu/~groberts/Papers/continuum.pdf), [Albouy–Kaloshin](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v176-n1-p10-p.pdf), [Tomar's special-mass paper](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00485v1), [Little](https://mathcs.holycross.edu/~little/JMMTalkCCs.pdf), [Nurowski](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.04136)
